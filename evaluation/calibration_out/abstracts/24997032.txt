The brain-gut axis in health and disease.

Al Omran Y(1), Aziz Q.

Author information:
(1)Centre for Digestive Diseases, Wingate Institute of Neurogastroenterology, 
Blizard Institute, Barts and The London School of Medicine and Dentistry, Queen 
Mary, University of London, London, UK.

The interaction between the brain and the gut has been recognized for many 
centuries. This bidirectional interaction occurs via neural, immunological and 
hormonal routes, and is important not only in normal gastrointestinal function 
but also plays a significant role in shaping higher cognitive function such as 
our feelings and our subconscious decision-making. Therefore, it remains 
unsurprising that perturbations in normal signalling have been associated with a 
multitude of disorders, including inflammatory and functional gastrointestinal 
disorders, and eating disorders.
