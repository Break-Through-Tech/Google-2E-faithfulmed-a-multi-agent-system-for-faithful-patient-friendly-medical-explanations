T wave abnormalities identify patients with previous lateral wall myocardial 
infarction and circumflex artery disease.

BACKGROUND: The diagnosis of previous lateral myocardial infarction is based on 
QRS morphology.
OBJECTIVES: To explore the diagnostic role of T wave abnormalities.
METHODS: We studied 166 patients with known or suspected ischemic heart disease 
who underwent a 12-lead electrocardiogram, myocardial perfusion scintigraphy, 
and coronary arteriography within 90days. We excluded patients with 
bundle-branch block, hypertrophy, or paced rhythm.
RESULTS: Only one patient had a prominent R wave in V1, no patient showed 
lateral Q waves of necrosis. T wave amplitude in V2-V6 ≥0.6mV, and T wave 
amplitude in lead 1+V6 ≤0mV detected a lateral infarction (sensitivity 33 and 
44%, specificity 83 and 80%). T wave amplitude in lead 1+V6 ≤0mV was the only 
independent predictor of infarction or LCx occlusion (AUC 0.72 and 0.74). Serum 
potassium values were not associated with T wave abnormalities.
CONCLUSION: T wave abnormalities identify previous lateral infarction and LCx 
disease.
